# Regions: free sources by country

A starter list, written from general knowledge and checked against the Open Archives API only.
Websites change. Before the first use in a project, open each site, read its terms, and note
the date in the research log. Replace or extend this file as the project learns more. Pull
requests with better regional notes are welcome.

Everything here is free to search. Some sites need a free account to see scans; agents never
log in for the user. Ask the user to run those searches and paste or export the results.

## How to add a region

Copy this block, fill it in, and keep it short:

```
### Country (code)
- Civil registration starts: year, and where the gaps are
- Church records: what survives and where it is held
- Census or population registers: years that exist
- Free indexes and scans: site, what it covers, how to query it
- Newspapers: site, period
- Name customs: patronymics, double surnames, calendar changes
- Terms and limits: API, rate limit, what is not allowed
```

## Netherlands, Belgium (Flanders, Wallonia)
- **Open Archives** (`openarchieven.nl`): about 99 percent Dutch records, with a small amount for
  Belgium, France and Suriname. API and script in this skill.
- **Delpher:** Dutch newspapers and books, free full text.
- **Oorlogsbronnen.nl:** the war years 1940 to 1945.
- **Regional archives** (BHIC, Erfgoed Leiden, Het Utrechts Archief, Noord-Hollands Archief and
  others) hold the scans behind the Open Archives index.
- **Belgium:** the State Archives (Search.arch.be and AGATHA) publish scans of parish and civil
  registers; Belgian civil registration begins in 1796.
- Dutch civil registration begins in 1811 for most of the country.

## United Kingdom and Ireland
- **FreeBMD, FreeCEN, FreeREG:** volunteer indexes of civil registration, census and church
  registers for England and Wales. No general API; read public results at human pace.
- **The National Archives Discovery catalogue:** free catalogue of records and where they are.
- **Welsh Newspapers Online:** free, to 1919.
- **Ireland:** irishgenealogy.ie for civil and many church records; the National Archives of Ireland
  census site for 1901 and 1911.
- Not allowed: subscription newspaper sites and the paid census sites.

## United States and Canada
- **FamilySearch:** the largest free collection, but it needs a login; use user-supplied results.
- **NARA catalog and census images,** **Library of Congress (Chronicling America** newspapers, with a free
  API), **HathiTrust, Internet Archive:** free.
- **Find a Grave and BillionGraves:** useful for leads; check the terms before collecting.
- **Library and Archives Canada** and provincial archives: free catalogues and some indexes.
- State and county records vary a great deal. Find out which courthouse holds the original.

## Germany, Austria, Switzerland
- There is no single national index. Look for **Matricula Online** (church books of many dioceses,
  with scans), regional state archives, **Archivportal-D** and the **Deutsche Digitale
  Bibliothek** (catalogues), **GenWiki** (a wiki guide to where records are), and local family
  books (Ortsfamilienbücher).
- Many church books exist only on subscription sites such as Archion; those are not allowed.
  Record them as leads.

## France
- **Departmental archives** (archives départementales) publish scans of parish and civil registers
  free of charge in most départements. Civil registration starts in 1792.
- **Geneanet** has free and paid parts. Use only what is free and open without a login.

## Nordic countries
- **Norway:** Digitalarkivet, free censuses and church books with scans.
- **Sweden:** Riksarkivet (part free); **Denmark:** the Danish Family Search and archive sites
  for censuses; **Finland:** HisKi for church records.
- Name customs: patronymics and farm names are common. Search by farm and parish, not only by surname.

## Poland and Central Europe
- **Geneteka** (a free index of births, marriages and deaths of Poland) and the state archives
  site **szukajwarchiwach.gov.pl** for scans. Borders changed; search the old administrative names.

## Australia and New Zealand
- **Trove** (Australia, with a public API) for newspapers and records, **Papers Past** (New Zealand
  newspapers). State registries offer paid certificates; index searches are often free.

## Name customs and calendars worth knowing everywhere

- Patronymics and farm names (Netherlands before 1811, Scandinavia, Wales, Frisia).
- Prefixes and compound names (van, de, von, Mac, O').
- The Gregorian calendar replaced the Julian one at different dates per country, from 1582
  in much of Catholic Europe to 1752 in Britain and 1918 in Russia.
- Spelling was not fixed. Search phonetically and with wildcards where the site allows.
